### Inversion Analysis

* **(a) Turning off the toolbar in production loses team feedback / comments:**
  * **Argument:** The team loses the ability to leave production-context comments and inspect live metadata. A narrower control—such as disabling the toolbar per-user via Vercel preferences or a cookie toggle—would preserve production feedback.
  * **Assessment:** **Does NOT beat the spec.** Section 2 deliberately scopes production as end-user-facing and designates Preview deployments for team comments (`"Leave Preview as it is (the toolbar is useful there for comments)"`). Desktop-centric shortcuts (`Cmd + .`) and cookie flags are impractical on mobile touchscreens where the floating element obstructs layout.

* **(b) Verification in Section 3 can pass while the operator still sees the overlay:**
  * **Argument:** In Section 3, Step 3 dictates testing in a `"new browser session"`. Because Section 1 establishes that anonymous users never see the toolbar, opening a new session (e.g. private/incognito window or cleared storage) drops Vercel team authentication, causing the toolbar to disappear regardless of whether the setting took effect. Step 4 is confounded if the preview deployment requires team auth or has separate environment rules. Furthermore, long-lived cookies, PWA standalone shells, or service-worker caching on the operator's actual device will continue rendering the overlay even after a third-party tester's verification passes.
  * **Assessment:** **BEATS the spec.** The verification procedure contains a false-positive loophole: an unauthenticated session trivially passes the "no toolbar" check.

  * **Argument:** Section 1 cites the app's internal feed toolbar (`.dashboard__feed-toolbar` at `site/src/components/app/mobile-feed-layout.observer.test.ts:69`) and Section 6 admits it was not established whether the overlay was the toolbar or the comments widget.   * **Assessment:** **Does NOT beat the spec.** Section 3 Step 1 explicitly checks for the Vercel `"toolbar circle"` (the draggable pill widget), and Section 6 correctly observes that comments ride the Vercel toolbar runtime anyway.
