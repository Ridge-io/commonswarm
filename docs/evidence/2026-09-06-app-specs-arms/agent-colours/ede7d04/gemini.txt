### Load-Bearing Design Choices & Inversions

#### 1. Server-side PostgREST filter vs. Client-side feed filtering
- **Inversion:** Filter signals client-side in `signal-feed.ts` across the loaded 25-row window, matching the All/Broadcast/Direct toggle.
- **Predicted Regression:** In channels where an entity posts infrequently, client filtering yields an empty feed despite active historical messages, requiring repeated client page fetches (re-introducing the P2 anti-pattern).
- **Beat the spec?** No. The spec's server-side `.eq("from", …).eq("from_kind", …)` correctly retrieves 25 matching rows directly from Postgres in a single query.

#### 2. Avatar click filters vs. Name click filters
- **Inversion:** Retain the chat plan's original phrasing where author names filter on click, keeping avatars decorative.
- **Predicted Regression:** Breaks existing app-wide behavior where `entityControl` on names opens the Entity Panel (7+ call sites), destroying inspection workflows and breaking existing tests.
- **Beat the spec?** No. Turning the avatar into a dedicated `<button>` for filtering while keeping name buttons for panel inspection preserves established behavior without modal interference.

#### 3. 3px left border + box-shadow fresh animation vs. Background row tinting
- **Inversion:** Tint `.dashboard__message` background with entity colour and keep `border-inline-start` solely for the fresh-row animation.
- **Predicted Regression:** Message background tinting ruins WCAG contrast in dark/light themes and creates severe visual clutter in multi-agent channels.
- **Beat the spec?** No. Shifting the fresh animation to an inset `box-shadow` allows the persistent 3px border to maintain contrast-compliant identity anchoring.

#### 4. Deterministic FNV-1a % 12 hashing vs. Dynamic session palette assignment
- **Inversion:** Dynamically assign unique palette indices 0..11 to active room participants to avoid color collisions.
- **Predicted Regression:** Participants recolor unpredictably across reloads, channels, and client devices (e.g. an agent appears cyan in one channel and rose in another).
- **Beat the spec?** No. Cross-session determinism is essential; persistent badges and names ensure identity when collisions occur (R8).
