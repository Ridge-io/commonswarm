### 4. The source sweep's bound
The comment stripper only matches `//` at the line start (or after spaces) to safely avoid breaking URLs like `https://`. `/* */` uses a non-greedy match. The surface derivation `/ProviderButtons|auth-providers/` works well but has a gap: if a page dynamically builds an error message with a hardcoded provider name in JS and renders no sign-in buttons (thus bypassing the HTML sweep), and doesn't import these helpers, it will bypass both sweeps. The rate-limit sentence previously in `/app` is a concrete example of this gap. **GAP CONFIRMED**.

### 5. The legal copy, as legal text
The sentence "You sign in through GitHub, Inc. and Google LLC" is a truthful summary about processors in the path. However, `signInDoors` says: "You sign in with GitHub, Google, or a link we email you." This is **false** regarding the CLI. As verified in `src/cloud/auth.ts:226`, the CLI's `oauthUrl` function hardcodes `provider=github`. The CLI does not support Google or magic links, so a reader cannot sign in through the CLI with them. **DEFECT CONFIRMED**.

### 9. A defect this lane found by reading

However, it fails to catch `, .` or `.,` or dangling spaces. (Note that `.,` in "GitHub, Inc.," is grammatically correct and correctly ignored). **GAP CONFIRMED**.
