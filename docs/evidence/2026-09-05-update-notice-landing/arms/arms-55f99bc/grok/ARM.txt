## Hardest attacks (round 4)

**`<live-dashboard>` as the “is this our page?” test.** A real `/app` deploy cannot lack it: `app.astro` renders `LiveDashboard`, and the built page is `<live-dashboard class="dashboard" …>`. Grep of `site/dist/` finds that tag only in `app/index.html`. A foreign same-origin Astro page *can* carry `/_astro/*.js|css` (measured: `/start` does) and does *not* carry the tag. That is the case `other-astro-page` builds. `if (!markup) return` at `LiveDashboard.astro:6595` is the right cut: `querySelector("live-dashboard")` will not match a comment or the string `</live-dashboard>`. I could not construct a same-origin 200 that has this element and is not the app.

**Dismissal reset.** `dismissedBuild = null` on `!isNewBuild` (`LiveDashboard.astro:6602-6608`) is the rollback fix. Clearing on “back in step” re-asks if B is served again. That is the point. Mixed-edge deploys can flap A/B and nag after a dismiss; that is the same state machine, not a second bug. `dismissedBuild` is `string | null`, not `""`. `servedBuild` is only set after both `served` and `markup` are non-empty, so the sentinel cannot collide.

**New observer cases.** `other-astro-page` strips the element, rewrites hashes, and asserts both (`update-notice.observer.test.ts:104-123`). Without the hash rewrite, `if (!served) return` would catch it and the case would not reach the new guard. `rollback` drives stage 2 (B) → dismiss → stage 3 (A) → stage 4 (B again). Removing only `dismissedBuild = null` leaves the bar down at stage 4 and fails `shown === true`. See §6 for a control gap that does **not** let that mutation through.
