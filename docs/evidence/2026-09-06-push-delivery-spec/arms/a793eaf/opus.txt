DEFECT 1. §2.1 / §0 / §2.4 — the signal TTL is never mentioned, and on the two
failure rows where the wake is lost the delivery is LOST, not late.

The claim. §2.1: "a lost wake … never a delivery". §0: "a lost wake costs at most
one reconcile interval and never a delivery." §2.4, "Missed wake" row, the
"why no delivery is lost" column: "the row is in signal_deliveries; the reconcile
claim reads the ledger", worst case "5 min latency". §2.4, "Trigger cannot send"
row: "exception swallowed; row inserted; wake lost", worst case "5 min", why no
delivery is lost: "W4's guard; the reconcile".

What is wrong. The ledger row existing is not sufficient for the reconcile to claim
it. Two things in claimAgentInbox turn on the signal's own TTL:

  supabase/functions/command/durable-delivery.ts:283 — the candidate CTE the
  reconcile claim runs carries `AND s.until > statement_timestamp()`. A delivery
  whose signal has expired is not a candidate.

  supabase/functions/command/durable-delivery.ts:212-231 — step 3 of the same call,
  before the candidate select, UPDATEs every unleased, unacked delivery of this
  principal whose `s.until <= statement_timestamp()` to
  `acked_at = statement_timestamp(), ack_outcome = 'expired'`. It is terminal.

And that TTL can be shorter than the reconcile interval, from a supported surface:

  supabase/functions/command/index.ts:1844 accepts `until_ms` at
  `integer(cmd.until_ms, 1)` — a floor of 1 millisecond — up to 30 days
  (SIGNAL_MAX_UNTIL_MS, :574).
  src/cli.ts:2565-2581 (signalDuration) accepts `--until 1m`: the regex is
  /^([1-9]\d*)(m|h|d)$/, so 60,000 ms is the smallest value a person can type.
  command/index.ts:8004 additionally clamps a thread reply to the ROOT's remaining
  TTL, so a reply into a thread whose root is nearly over inherits a horizon of
  seconds without anyone typing one.

So: `cswarm ask --until 1m <agent> "…"`. The delivery row commits. The wake is
dropped by Realtime, or the trigger's `EXCEPTION WHEN OTHERS THEN RETURN NULL`
swallowed a send failure — both rows §2.4 already concedes. Nothing else claims for
300 s. At the reconcile, step 3 terminalizes the row as `expired` and the candidate
select excludes it. The listener never sees the signal. The sender's receipt says
expired. Under today's 2 s poll and under lane A's 15 s poll the same signal is
delivered with room to spare; the design's 150x widening of the only fallback
interval is what turns a latency into a loss.

The document never mentions `signals.until`, `until_ms`, `SIGNAL_DEFAULT_UNTIL_MS`
or `ack_outcome = 'expired'` anywhere — I grepped it. No earlier arm raised it
either (no file under arms/ mentions s.until or until_ms). It is in the ask's scope
("failure modes … missed wakeups") and it falsifies the sentence the whole design
rests on, so it belongs in §2.4 with a bound, not in §9 as a deferral.

What would settle it, for the author (not a redesign): the reconcile interval has to
be bounded by the shortest live TTL the listener could be waiting on. The cheapest
honest forms are (a) make LISTENER_RECONCILE_POLL_MS the ceiling and let the read
that already runs each reconcile return the nearest `until` among pending rows, so
the next deadline is min(reconcileDueAt, nearestUntil - margin); or (b) state a
floor on `until_ms` at the edge above the reconcile interval and say so in §2.4 and
§3; or (c) if neither is wanted, say plainly in §2.1 and in both §2.4 rows that a
signal whose TTL is shorter than the reconcile interval CAN be lost to a dropped
wake, and put the bound in §9. What cannot stand is the current pairing of a 300 s
fallback with "never a delivery".

DEFECT 2. §2.2 W3.1 / §6 L2 — the spec points L2's DROP/CREATE at the superseded
body of agent_delivery_read_context, and calls the current one a re-grant.

The claim. W3.1: "its only reach into principals is the SECURITY DEFINER function
