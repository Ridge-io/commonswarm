## 2. Inversion Arguments on Draft-2 Changes

### (a) Inversion: Rotating the wake ID on revocation does not make revocation complete

* **Inversion Hypothesis:** Rotating `agent_principals.wake_id` upon token/principal revocation leaves an open socket or race condition whereby an adversary with a revoked credential still observes wakes or claims deliveries.
* **Interleaving Analysis:**
  1. *Eavesdropping on old topic:* Assume adversary holds revoked token $T_1$ and maintains an active websocket joined to `cswarm-wake:{wake_id_old}`. The revocation transaction atomically marks $T_1$ revoked in `swarm.agent_tokens` and updates `swarm.agent_principals.wake_id` to `wake_id_new`. When a new delivery arrives, trigger `swarm.wake_agent_delivery()` (W4) reads the current `agent_principals.wake_id` (`wake_id_new`) and calls `realtime.send(..., 'cswarm-wake:' || wake_id_new, true)`. Realtime broadcast routes strictly by exact topic name. The adversary receives zero messages on `cswarm-wake:{wake_id_old}`.
  2. *Re-joining on new topic:* To join `cswarm-wake:{wake_id_new}`, the adversary must obtain `wake_id_new`. Under W3, `wake_id` is only returned in the response payloads of `read` (inbox), `claim_agent_inbox`, `mint_agent_token`, and `renew_agent_token`. All four endpoints validate the caller's bearer token against `agent_tokens`; because $T_1$ is revoked, these endpoints return 401/403. Even if `wake_id_new` (32 cryptographically random bytes = 256 bits entropy) could be guessed, policy W5 (`swarm.wake_topic_authorized`) checks `agent_tokens_by_principal` for the existence of an active, unrevoked token. If the principal was revoked or has no remaining active tokens, the policy returns `false`, causing Realtime to reject the join with `CHANNEL_ERROR: Unauthorized`.
  3. *Racing delivery claims:* Suppose a wake was broadcast to `wake_id_old` a millisecond before revocation committed. The adversary receives `{ "v": 1, "signal_id": "<uuid>", "enqueued_at": "<timestamptz>" }`. This payload contains no message body, payload data, or sender. To claim the delivery, the adversary must execute `POST /functions/v1/command` (`claim_agent_inbox`). At step 1 (`command/index.ts:8352`), the token authentication and principal validation reject the revoked token before any claim can occur.
* **Verdict on Inversion (a):** **REFUTED (Did not beat spec).** Revocation of delivery visibility is complete at the instant the rotation transaction commits.

### (b) Inversion: A per-principal wake ID with two listeners on one credential loses or duplicates deliveries

* **Inversion Hypothesis:** Because `wake_id` is per-principal rather than per-listener or per-token, two concurrent listeners ($L_1$ and $L_2$) sharing a credential will race upon receiving the same wake broadcast, causing duplicate delivery claims or message loss.
* **Concurrency Analysis:**
  1. *Duplicate deliveries:* When a wake fires on `cswarm-wake:{wake_id}`, both $L_1$ and $L_2$ wake and issue `POST /functions/v1/command` (`claim_agent_inbox`). In the database (`durable-delivery.ts:183-189, 272-300`):
     - Step 1 executes `SELECT ... FROM swarm.agent_principals ... FOR UPDATE`, strictly serializing concurrent claim transactions for the same principal.
     - The first claim transaction ($L_1$) selects candidate rows with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED LIMIT 1`, updates `lease_id = gen_random_uuid()` and `leased_until = statement_timestamp() + 30s`, and returns the leased delivery.
     - The second claim transaction ($L_2$) executes immediately after. It finds no rows satisfying `acked_at IS NULL AND lease_id IS NULL`, returning `delivery_refs = []`.
     - Because leases are exclusive and guarded by database transactions, $L_2$ never receives the row claimed by $L_1$. No duplicate delivery occurs.
  2. *Lost deliveries:*
     - If $L_1$ crashes while holding the lease, the lease expires after `DELIVERY_LEASE_MS` (30 s). On the next reconcile tick (or subsequent wake), step 2 of `claimAgentInbox` resets expired leases (`lease_id = NULL`), allowing $L_2$ (or restarted $L_1$) to claim and process the delivery.
     - If a wake broadcast is dropped by the network or Realtime, the delivery row remains recorded in `swarm.signal_deliveries` (`acked_at IS NULL`). The periodic reconcile tick (every 5 minutes while subscribed, or 15 s if disconnected) executes `claim_agent_inbox` directly against the database table.
     - If two signals ($S_1, S_2$) arrive concurrently, $L_1$ claims $S_1$ and $L_2$ claims $S_2$, or $L_1$ claims $S_1$ and drains $S_2$ on its subsequent loop tick.
* **Verdict on Inversion (b):** **REFUTED (Did not beat spec).** "Push is a hint. The row is the truth." Postgres locks and leases guarantee delivery exclusivity and prevent loss.

### (c) Inversion: Keeping a read on the 5-minute reconcile is either insufficient for the standing-grant use stamp or makes the cost claim wrong

* **Inversion Hypothesis:** Retaining `readAgentSignalPage` every 5 minutes either fails to keep standing grants from self-suspending, or invalidates the cost reductions calculated in sections 1.3, 7, and 8.
* **Verification & Arithmetic Recomputation:**
  1. *Standing-grant use stamp sufficiency:* Under `supabase/migrations/20260901000001_standing_grants.sql:199-207`, standing grants self-suspend only after 14 days without activity (`interval '14 days'`). A reconcile read executed every 5 minutes executes $24 \times 12 = 288$ times per day ($4,032$ times per 14-day window). This provides a $\sim 4,000\times$ safety margin against idle suspension.
  2. *Cost calculations:*
     - **Section 1.3 (Baseline today):** Measured wall-clock tick $\approx 3.6$ s yields 24,000 ticks/day. $24,000 \text{ reads} + 24,000 \text{ claims} + 5,760 \text{ activity} = 53,760 \approx 53,800$ invocations/day. Consistent.
     - **Section 7 (Post-spec idle seat-day):**
       - Reconcile ticks: $1,440 \text{ min} / 5 \text{ min} = 288$.
       - Reconcile edge invocations: $288 \text{ reads} + 288 \text{ claims} = 576$.
       - Heartbeat: $1,440 \text{ min} \times 1/\text{min} = 1,440$.
       - Renewals: $24$. Wake claims: $\approx 100$. Drains: $\approx 50$.
       - Total: $576 + 1,440 + 24 + 100 + 50 = 2,190 \approx 2,200$ invocations/day.
       - With watcher: $2,190 + 288 \text{ watcher reconcile reads} = 2,478$ invocations/day.
       - Rows written: Reconcile claims (288) + wake claims ($\approx 100$) write to `rate_buckets` (per-minute bucket). Total $\approx 400$ rows, purged within 2 hours ($0$ surviving past 2 h).
       - At 10,000 seats with 15-min reconcile: $10,000 \times (96 \text{ reads} + 96 \text{ claims} + 24 \text{ renewals} + 150 \text{ wakes/drains} + 97 \text{ watcher}) = 4.63\text{ M} \approx 4.6\text{ M}$ invocations/day.
     - **Section 8 (10-minute idle measurement window):**
       - Reconcile: $10 \text{ min} / 5 \text{ min} = 2 \text{ ticks} \implies 2 \text{ reads} + 2 \text{ claims} = 4$.
       - Activity: $10 \text{ min} \times 1/\text{min} = 10$.
       - Total: $2 + 2 + 10 = 14$ invocations in 10 minutes ($1.4/\text{min}$).
       - Rows written: $2 \text{ rate-bucket rows}$, $0$ kept past 2 hours.
* **Verdict on Inversion (c):** **REFUTED (Did not beat spec).** Reconcile reads are fully and accurately budgeted across sections 0, 1.3, 7, and 8, and guarantee grant freshness.

### (d) Inversion: A volatile DEFAULT on `agent_principals.wake_id` is not additive for a 0.1.56 seat at migration time

* **Inversion Hypothesis:** Adding `wake_id text NOT NULL DEFAULT translate(encode(gen_random_bytes(32), 'base64'), '+/=', '-_')` violates additivity, causing migration failures or errors for deployed 0.1.56 clients/functions.
* **Schema & Additivity Analysis:**
  1. *PostgreSQL migration execution:* Because `gen_random_bytes()` is volatile, Postgres evaluates the expression for each existing row in `swarm.agent_principals`. Every existing principal is immediately populated with a unique 32-byte (43-character base64url) identifier. The subsequent `CREATE UNIQUE INDEX agent_principals_wake_id` succeeds without collision.
  2. *Unmodified `INSERT` statements:* Existing edge functions (e.g. `mint_agent_token`) do not specify `wake_id` in their `INSERT INTO swarm.agent_principals` column lists. Postgres automatically assigns the generated default value. No deployed INSERT statement fails.
  3. *Client compatibility (0.1.56):* 0.1.56 clients do not query Postgres directly and ignore unfamiliar fields in `/functions/v1/read` and `/functions/v1/command` JSON payloads (`src/cloud/command-client.ts:717-735`, `src/cloud/signals.ts:1211-1218`). 0.1.56 clients continue their polling loop unimpeded.
* **Verdict on Inversion (d):** **REFUTED (Did not beat spec).** The column definition is fully backward-compatible and additive.
