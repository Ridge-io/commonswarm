## 2. View recreation — not refuted

Two-step splice: split on top-level `FROM swarm.signals`, then the first `WHERE` in that tail. Attachments and the new recipients subquery sit in the select list (`FROM swarm.signal_attachments` / `signal_recipients`), so they do not steal the split.

Wrap is `(old predicate) OR (new disjunct)`. Live WHERE is `is_member AND (undirected OR to_user OR owns_agent)`. That becomes `is_member AND (old audience OR new audience)`.

New disjunct has its own `is_member`. A non-member cannot pass. `EXISTS` names `auth.uid()` as a user recipient or as owner of a named agent. Author and bystander stay out unless they are in the set.

`assert_view_clauses_preserved` is vacuous for the new marker on this run (`p_before` does not have it). The positive control after recreation requires `AS recipients` and `recipient_user_id = auth.uid()`. Widening is not that helper’s job; the before/after visibility tests are.

`@@SWARM_SPLIT@@` / `@@SWARM_WHERE@@` are not in the live view text. A later top-level `AND` restriction would not apply to the new disjunct after a wrap; that is a future splice hazard, not a defect in this body.

## 3. First-recipient guarantee — not refuted

`resolveSignalWriteTarget`: `to` present → every entry checked live, entry 0 becomes the scalar, `inReplyTo` null.

`postSignal` writes one side-table row per `command.to` entry, positions from `.entries()`. No `to` writes no rows.

Deferred trigger: positions `0..n-1`, position 0 `IS NOT DISTINCT FROM` the scalar pair. Scalar-only posts never fire it (that is the no-backfill path). Append-only blocks a later scalar change.

`in_reply_to`: validator refuses `to` beside it (`!addressedByList`). Re-address still uses the parent author as the one scalar. No side-table rows. Thread reply and `working-on` refuse `to` the same way they refuse the scalar fields.

Gap, not a guarantee break: `addressedToCaller` still reads only the parent’s scalar columns. A second recipient can read the parent and still get 403 on `in_reply_to`. The lane does not claim otherwise.

No path I found stores recipient rows with a mismatched or null scalar and commits.
