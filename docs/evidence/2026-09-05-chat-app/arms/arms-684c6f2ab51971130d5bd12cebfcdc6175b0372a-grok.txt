Confirmed, not assumed. `page.rows[0]` is newest; `page.rows.at(-1)` is the oldest of the displayed 25.

**Is `page.hasMore` the right test for “the page is the whole history”?** Yes. `hasMore === false` means the replica returned fewer than 26 rows, so nothing has rolled off the first page. The floor is then `""` and every pruned, on-screen, not-fetched stand-in is kept. That includes: an empty channel; a channel with 1–25 messages; and the lag case where the replica has 25 and is missing this browser’s post (25 ≠ 26, so `hasMore` is false). Showing 25 replica rows plus the stand-in is the whole history the replica knows, plus the write it has not caught. `hasMore === true` means a 26th row exists; the displayed 25 is a window; the floor is the oldest of those 25.

Using `page.rows.length === 25` instead would be wrong: a complete history of exactly 25 would look like a window and drop a lagging post older than the 25th, even though there is no page 2.

**Is `>=` right at the floor, given the query’s tie-break on id?** For the lag this record exists for, yes. A just-posted row is newer than the 25th the replica already has, so `createdAt > pageFloor` and it is kept. `>=` (not `>`) is the equal-timestamp lag case at the floor: a concurrent post missing from the replica still stands in.

The query’s full order is `(created_at DESC, id DESC)`. The filter does not ask `id`. A rolled-off row with `createdAt === pageFloor` and `id <` the 25th row’s id would be kept and sorted to the 26th slot. I tried to make that a finding. To see it you need a full first page, a stand-in that shares the 25th row’s millisecond, and a smaller id, all inside the 30s grace. Replica lag of a just-posted row sits near `page.rows[0]`, not at the 25th. Page roll of that same row is almost always `createdAt < pageFloor` and is dropped. The remaining hole over-includes one same-millisecond row at the bottom of the page; it does not drop a lagging post and it does not resurrect one as newest. Load older’s `known` set then skips it. I am not failing the SHA on it. The observer pins `>= pageFloor` without id (`chat-channels.observer.test.ts:464–478`); that is the same enshrinement shape as `newestFetched`, pointed at a comparison that does not drop the row the last FAIL named.
