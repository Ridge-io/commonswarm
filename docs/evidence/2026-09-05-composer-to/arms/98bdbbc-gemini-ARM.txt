## 1. A SEVENTH INSTANCE OF THE SAME CLASS
I am attacking the ruling from Grok in the previous round about a draft with a body. The ruling claims that adding a returning member back to a draft's To: row would be a "hidden recipient" because the reader never chose them. But this logic is flawed: the reader WOULD SEE the returned member on the To: row! The row exists specifically so the reader can see the address and verify who it goes to.

The real issue is that the prune upon arrival was SILENT. The reader never "chose" to remove the member; the system removed them silently on arrival. Then, the system permanently records this silent prune as a deliberate "choice" if the body happens to be non-empty. This means a member who returns is permanently excluded from a draft, even though the user was just continuing to type. Thus, a draft with a body still separates the pair from what a fresh derivation would do, treating a transition residue (the silent prune) as a choice. This is still the same class of bug.

## 5. THE STORED DRAFT'S NEW REACH
Yes, it can resurrect a sent message! When a send succeeds, but the reader switches workspace while it is in-flight, `submit` early-returns from the `try` block (`LiveDashboard.astro:7663`) and skips the `clearComposerDraft()` call. The `finally` block also early-returns (`LiveDashboard.astro:7812`) and skips its own `clearComposerDraft()` call. The draft for the old workspace is therefore left in `localStorage`, retaining the sent text. When the reader returns to the old workspace, the message they already successfully sent is resurrected as an active draft.
